## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics | People who got through a hard stretch with Fable leave the acute phase with skills they can use on their own and a plan for next time. When stress returns, they’re ready sooner, and Fable is the first thing they reach for, not something they have to rediscover.|
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | Premium and trial users aged 25–45 in the US, UK and Canada who engaged deeply during an acute period (for example, 30+ sessions in their first 90 days) and are now in the day 90–150 window, when they’re most likely to quietly leave. Mobile app only. Explicitly out of scope this year: free-only casual users, new-user acquisition, non-English markets, B2B/employer, and web. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | Shift Fable from relief to resilience through two things competitors can’t easily copy.
Skill tracks: short, CBT-informed but non-clinical progressions (for example Sleep that holds, Catching stress early, Steady through change) built from the meditation and sleep content users already trust. They have a clear finish line and visible mastery, so continuing feels like progress, not maintenance.
A personal “what worked for me” toolkit: assembled from each user’s own acute-phase history (the sessions, times and techniques that actually helped), plus an easy way back in when life gets hard again.
The moat is that a new AI app can personalize, but it can’t know what got this person through their worst month. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | *Build:* a skill-track and progression system; the personal toolkit; a rules-based transition trigger. Start with simple rules (session drop-off after a deep-engagement period) rather than ML. Also build a calm re-entry experience for lapsed users.*Repurpose:* the content team re-sequences and lightly re-records the existing library into tracks. Net-new content stays under about 20% of effort.*Partner:* clinical advisors to design the CBT-informed skills and keep claims firmly on the wellness side of the regulatory line, plus a legal review of all outcome language.*Buy:* experimentation and lifecycle-messaging tools.*Team shape:* two squads of about six (Progression; Toolkit and Re-entry), with clinical advisory and research shared across both. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | *North star:* premium subscribers from the deep-engager cohort who are still subscribed at month 12.
*Leading indicators:* skill-track starts and completions, toolkit saves, reactivation after a lapse.
Guardrails: notification opt-outs, session spikes that suggest distress rather than practice (routed to the safety protocol), refund and complaint rates, and trust scores.
*Rituals:* weekly experiment review; monthly cohort review that includes exit interviews with users who left (separating “I’m fine now” from “Fable had nothing next for me”); quarterly scale, adjust or stop decision.
*Kill criterion:* if month-6 retention in the test cohort isn’t measurably ahead of control by the end of Q1 2027, return to the board with a different bet.|

## 2. Your one hard no

We will not optimize for daily engagement. That means no streaks, daily goals, loss-aversion nudges, or DAU targets for this initiative. It costs something real: it overrides the growth team’s instincts, removes the easiest lever for short-term retention, and means dashboards will look quieter than investors are used to. But Fable’s brand is “the app that actually helped.” Guilt-driven engagement would trade that trust for a few months of usage, and trust is the only thing in this strategy a competitor can’t buy.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone._

> **Objective:** Turn Fable’s most valuable users from people who quietly leave into people who move on to building skills and stay subscribed because Fable keeps being useful.
>
> - **KR1:** Increase month-6 premium retention in the deep-engager cohort by 15 percentage points over the current baseline (to be confirmed before launch).
> - **KR2:** [Increase the share of the cohort, including users who have stopped opening the app, who report they can handle a stressful week using what they learned on Fable. Survey at day 150, with a target of +20 points. Including lapsed users removes the survivorship bias in your current KR3.
> - **KR3:** Double the share of lapsed deep engagers who return to Fable within 12 months, measured as reactivation through the toolkit or re-entry flow.
