# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> The hardest part was choosing between several reasonable ideas rather than identifying one obvious answer. More content, AI check-ins, community features and clinical programs could all create value, but they did not support the same strategy. I worked through this by narrowing the target to deeply engaged users in the day 90–150 window, defining the outcome we wanted, and testing whether each roadmap item connected to the OKRs. This exposed that some of my original Rocks, such as accessibility and performance improvements, were important work but were not the strategic bets that would solve the retention problem.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

> My biggest learning was that strategy is as much about what you choose not to do as what you fund. A roadmap can look sensible while still being disconnected from the strategy if it is mainly a list of useful features and technical improvements. I also learned that financial assumptions need to use consistent units. In the team-tier case, using individual CAC against team revenue initially made the payback period look much better than it really was. Finally, a kill criterion is only useful when it names the metric, threshold, timeline and consequence in advance.

## "Aha!" moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

> My main aha moment was realizing that Fable should not solve retention by trying to make users engage every day. The stronger opportunity was to help users move from short-term relief to lasting resilience and become the first thing they return to when stress reappears. That protects the trust Fable has already earned and gives the product a clearer reason to exist after the acute need has passed.
